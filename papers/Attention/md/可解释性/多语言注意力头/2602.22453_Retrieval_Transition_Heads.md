# Bridging Latent Reasoning and Target-Language Generation via Retrieval-Transition Heads

**Authors**: Shaswat Patel, Vishvesh Trivedi, Yue Han, Yihuai Hong, Eunsol Choi

**Year**: 2026

**arXiv ID**: 2602.22453

**Status**: ⚠️ Withdrawn by author (Yihuai Hong) due to "many statements that are still unclear and lack sufficient justification"

## Abstract
Recent work has identified a subset of attention heads in Transformer as retrieval heads, which are responsible for retrieving information from the context. In this work, we first investigate retrieval heads in multilingual contexts. In multilingual language models, we find that retrieval heads are often shared across multiple languages. Expanding the study to cross-lingual setting, we identify Retrieval-Transition Heads (RTH) — a new category of attention heads that govern the transition to specific target-language output. We demonstrate that RTHs are distinct from retrieval heads and more vital for Chain-of-Thought reasoning in multilingual LLMs. Experiments on Qwen-2.5 and Llama-3.1 across four multilingual tasks (MMLU-ProX, MGSM, MLQA, XQuaD) show that masking RTH induces bigger performance drop than masking Retrieval Heads (RH).

## Key Contribution
- Identifies **Retrieval-Transition Heads** as a distinct category of attention heads for cross-lingual output
- Shows retrieval heads are often **shared across multiple languages** in multilingual LMs
- Demonstrates RTH are more critical for CoT reasoning than standard retrieval heads

## Relevance
Directly related to the RQ topic of language-specific vs language-general attention heads. The paper suggests a tripartite taxonomy: language-general retrieval heads (shared across languages) + retrieval-transition heads (language-specific output gating) + other heads.
